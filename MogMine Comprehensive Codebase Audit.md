## **Mobile-First Futures Trade Logging, Review Workflows, and Behavioral Design**

### **Mobile Ergonomics, Thumb-Zone Geometry, and Adaptive Layouts**

Retail trading has shifted decisively toward mobile execution and review. According to industry estimates synthesized in an *Oxford Journal* review (citing SQ Magazine, 2025, and CFA Institute, 2023), retail investors generate roughly 20% of daily US equity trading volume, Gen-Z and Millennials account for over 60% of retail trading activity, and 65% of Gen-Z alongside 55% of Millennial investors manage accounts primarily via mobile apps6. Designing a mobile-first futures journal therefore demands physical ergonomics engineered for touch rather than shrunk desktop interfaces. Field observations by UX researcher Steven Hoober found that 49% of mobile users operate phones one-handed, 36% cradle the device in one hand while tapping with the other, and 15% use two hands, while designer Josh Clark estimated that roughly 75% of mobile interactions are thumb-driven4. Crucially, Hoober's subsequent measurements warned against treating the static thumb-sweep arc as rigid truth because users constantly shift grips; tap accuracy degrades toward screen perimeters, requiring physical target dimensions of roughly 7 mm at center, 9 mm along edges, and 12 mm in corners4.  
Because MIT Touch Lab measurements cited in mobile engineering guidance place average fingertip width at 16–20 mm and thumb pads near 25 mm, shipping bare-minimum targets produces chronic mis-taps across dense futures capture forms39. Platform guidelines mandate hit areas of at least 44×44 pt on iOS and 48×48 dp (\~9 mm, with 8 dp minimum separation) on Android, whereas WCAG 2.2 sets 24×24 CSS pixels strictly as a legal accessibility floor—a baseline carrying formal regulatory force under the European Accessibility Act since June 28, 20254. MogMine's mobile layout system should anchor primary capture controls ("Log Fill," "Commit Thesis," "Save Review") in the bottom 40% of the screen while relegating destructive actions ("Delete Trade," "Reset Account") to hard-to-reach top corners39. Visual icons can remain compact (20–24 px) provided their invisible tappable hit regions expand to at least 44–48 px, paired with practitioner spacing tokens of 8 pt between ≥44 pt targets, 12 pt between 32–44 pt targets, and 16 pt whenever an interactive control sits within 80 pt of a screen edge39. To verify ergonomic quality beyond static sizing checks, mobile telemetry should track rage-tap rate (two or more taps within 500 ms inside a 40 pt radius), edge-tap collision rate (within 16 pt of left/right system gesture margins), and tap-to-visual-feedback latency kept strictly under 100 ms39.  
Across tablet and desktop viewports, MogMine should reject stretched single-column mobile layouts in favor of Material Design 3 canonical adaptive layouts mapped across Compact, Medium, Expanded, Large, and Extra-Large breakpoints41. Trade logs and review queues align with the **List-Detail** pattern (a fixed-width list paired with a flexible chart/detail canvas at Expanded and wider breakpoints), while active trade inspection fits the **Supporting Pane** pattern—presenting execution metadata, process grades, and agent annotations inside a docked bottom sheet on Compact/Medium screens and as a co-planar 360 dp trailing pane on Expanded displays, simultaneously converting the mobile bottom bar into a vertical navigation rail to preserve vertical candlestick space41.

### **Cognitive Load, Progressive Disclosure, and Hybrid Input Controls**

Intraday futures traders often log entries or exits immediately after volatile stops or fast winners, when affective arousal narrows working memory. Although George Miller (1956) originally proposed a short-term memory span of 7±2 chunks, Nelson Cowan's (2001) cognitive psychology reassessment refined active working-memory capacity to roughly 4 chunks47. Simultaneously, Hick's Law demonstrates that decision time scales with the number of simultaneous choices displayed, and Cognitive Load Theory (Sweller, 1988\) shows that extraneous interface complexity competes directly with task reasoning50. Exposing contract selection, side, quantity, entry/stop/exit prices, session tags, playbook IDs, mistake taxonomies, emotional state, and screenshot uploaders simultaneously on a mobile trade sheet creates cognitive overload and drives journaling abandonment precisely on tilt days when honest logging matters most.  
Applying Nielsen Norman Group's four cognitive-load form principles—Structure, Transparency, Clarity, and Support—yields a disciplined two-level progressive disclosure architecture50. Practitioner UX guidance citing Jakob Nielsen's "workbench test" recommends that users complete roughly 80% of core tasks on Level 1 without opening secondary drawers, while keeping total disclosure depth strictly to two levels because each additional layer multiplies clicks and halves discoverability51. On Level 1, MogMine should cluster essential execution facts in logical conversational order (Account & Instrument, Side & Quantity, Entry/Stop/Exit, and Pre-Trade Thesis/Playbook), using persistent visible labels instead of disappearing low-contrast placeholders that users confuse for prefilled defaults53. Navigation and filtering must favor recognition over recall by keeping active filters, recent instruments, and playbook chips visible rather than tucked inside hamburger menus or free-text autocomplete boxes55. Level 2 should hold conditional or situational inputs—partial scale-outs, manual commission overrides, scheduled macro-catalyst flags, and custom tags—behind clearly labeled controls rather than mystery-meat icons51.  
Numeric controls must match the mathematical nature of each field. Per Nielsen Norman Group's input-control guidelines, **text-field steppers** (an editable numeric field flanked by large −/+ buttons with long-press continuous acceleration and desktop arrow-key support) fit discrete contract quantities anchored near a modal default (such as 1–5 MES contracts), but perform poorly for continuous or wide-ranging futures prices59. For stop-loss and profit-target specification, MogMine should pair coarse visual exploration (dragging horizontal price lines on the chart) with fine numerical entry (tick-snapped price inputs) synchronized within 0.1 s62. On desktop review sessions, MogMine should provide keyboard-first parity without hurting novice learnability: following Nielsen Norman Group's UI accelerator guidelines and power-user patterns seen in tools like Linear and Superhuman, high-frequency queue operations should bind J/K (next/previous trade), Enter (open detail), ⌘/Ctrl+K (search/command palette), E (edit review), Esc (dismiss), and ? (discoverable shortcut reference), while never overriding universal OS/browser bindings63. Accelerators should be surfaced unobtrusively inline within menus, via hover tooltips on desktop, and through contextual hints after repeated manual clicks, always paired with immediate confirmation feedback and an Undo snackbar for error recovery66.

### **Behavioral Finance Safeguards, Anti-Bias Review, and Privacy by Design**

A trade journal that foregrounds raw dollar P\&L reinforces the exact cognitive biases that degrade trading performance. Three empirical behavioral mechanisms demand explicit structural defenses in MogMine's review architecture. First, **outcome bias** and **hindsight bias** systematically corrupt post-trade self-grading. In Jonathan Baron and John Hershey's (1988) foundational experiments, subjects judging identical decisions made under known probabilities rated decisions significantly higher when the chance outcome succeeded than when it failed (mean paired gap 0.70, t(19) \= 4.04, p \< .001)2. A 2023 replication across 692 participants (Aiyer et al.) reported gaps of 0.77 SD for a decision-maker's own choice and 1.10 SD for an advisor's choice, persisting even among reviewers who explicitly stated outcomes should not influence evaluation3. Compounded by Baruch Fischhoff's (1975) hindsight bias ("creeping determinism"), traders examining finished charts convince themselves that a stochastic reversal was obvious ex ante67. Because conscious resolve fails to neutralize outcome bias, MogMine must enforce structural debiasing: lock a timestamped pre-trade thesis and initial stop before trade resolution, provide a **Blind Review Mode** that masks realized P\&L, R-multiple, and post-exit bars until process grading is saved, and frame performance feedback around multi-trade batches rather than single outcomes3.  
Second, MogMine must avoid consumer-brokerage gamification patterns classified by the SEC as Digital Engagement Practices (DEPs)6. In an experimental study by the UK Financial Conduct Authority (Gathergood et al., 2024; over 9,000 participants), DEPs elevated trading frequency and risk-taking—especially among investors aged 18–34—while observational FCA analyses associated high-DEP platforms with weaker investor outcomes (noting that isolating direct causation remains difficult due to user self-selection)6. Complementary experimental work by Hüller, Reimann, and Warren (2023; six experiments, 3,776 participants) found that gamified interfaces introduce a competing goal of "winning" that systematically increases risk-taking, and Barber et al. (2022) showed that attention-induced mobile prompts on Robinhood predicted negative subsequent returns6. Similarly, randomized experiments by Chapkovski, Khapko, and Zoican (2024) found that hedonic gamification features (confetti, achievement badges) lifted trading volume by 5.17%, even though roughly 70% of the broader activity gap between gamified and non-gamified platforms reflected self-selection6. Combined with a 2025 FINRA survey (cited via Wang & Lee, 2026\) showing that 62% of investors under age 35 believe they must take big risks, 43% trade options, and 29% buy meme stocks, introducing celebratory animations, streaks, or urgent push alerts into a leveraged futures app is behaviorally hazardous6. Grounding retention instead in Ryan and Deci's (2000) **Self-Determination Theory**—supporting Autonomy (volitional custom rubrics), Competence (objective execution feedback without demeaning judgment), and Relatedness (opt-in peer/coach critique)—and Amber Case's **Calm Technology** principles (exception-based risk alerts only upon breaching daily loss or tilt thresholds, peripheral status indicators, and optional cool-down friction during volatile losing streaks) promotes sustainable mastery without inducing introjected guilt or overtrading6.  
Third, MogMine must prevent **mode errors** and embed **Privacy by Design**. Don Norman's human-factors research identifies mode errors as design failures caused when identical controls behave differently across hidden states73. Traders switching between Live Funded accounts, Prop Evaluations, Sim/Paper accounts, and Drill Replay must see persistent, unmistakable visual headers and explicit text badges naming the active mode so sim logs never contaminate live ledgers. In parallel, Ann Cavoukian's 7 Foundational Principles of Privacy by Design—particularly *Privacy as the Default*—justify a one-tap **Discreet Mode** (established across modern fintech apps) that masks dollar balances and dollar P\&L in favor of normalized R-multiples across both live screens and external card exports, defaulting to dollar redaction whenever a trader shares charts externally75.

## **Dark-Mode Visual Hierarchy, Color Accessibility, and lightweight-charts v5 Canvas Architecture**

### **Dark-Mode Optical Ergonomics and APCA vs. WCAG 2.2 Verification**

Futures traders spend hours scanning dark charts in dim environments, making dark-theme calibration a core usability requirement rather than a cosmetic preference. Poorly engineered dark interfaces fail simultaneously in two directions: placing pure white (\#FFFFFF) on pure black (\#000000) triggers **optical halation** (bright letterforms appearing to bleed and blur into dark voids, particularly for readers with astigmatism), while flattened surface grays erase depth because drop shadows become invisible against near-black canvases10. Material Design and modern dark-UI architecture replace shadows with stepped surface luminance ramps (making cards, sidebars, and modals progressively lighter than the base canvas) reinforced by subtle 1 px hairline borders (rgba(255,255,255,0.08)) and desaturated, luminance-boosted semantic accents that do not visually vibrate81.  
Critically, relying solely on legacy WCAG 2.x contrast ratios fails in dark mode. WCAG 2.x computes luminance contrast symmetrically regardless of polarity and, according to APCA creator Andrew Somers, can overestimate perceived contrast for very dark colors by as much as 200% to 250%—approving dark-mode muted body text that is practically unreadable8. The Accessible Perceptual Contrast Algorithm (APCA), under ongoing evaluation as a candidate perceptual contrast model for future WCAG 3.0 drafts, outputs polarity-aware Lightness Contrast (Lc) scores calibrated to font size and weight (targeting roughly \|Lc\| ≥ 75 for body prose, \|Lc\| ≥ 60 for short labels/axis text, and \|Lc\| ≈ 90 for crisp headings without glare)8. Because WCAG 2.2 AA (4.5:1 normal text, 3:1 large text and meaningful non-text graphics) remains the legally enforceable standard under ADA, EAA, and EN 301 549 while WCAG 3.0's final contrast algorithm remains undecided as of April 2026, MogMine should implement **dual compliance** (aligned with the BridgePCA approach): every color pair must satisfy both WCAG 2.2 AA and APCA perceptual targets9.

### **Colorblind-Safe Trading Charts, Financial Typography, and Data-Pixel Discipline**

WCAG's "Use of Color" rule prohibits using hue as the sole visual channel for directional states, and WCAG 2.1 mandates at least 3:1 contrast for meaningful graphical components—a threshold thin candle wicks routinely miss on dark backgrounds11. Rather than locking traders into red/green candlesticks, MogMine should provide a named colorblind-safe preset using high-luminance Okabe-Ito sky blue (\#56B4E9) and orange (\#E69F00) on dark canvases—since standard dark Okabe-Ito blue (\#0072B2) collapses to 3.69:1 / Lc \-26.3 on \#0d0f17—alongside full CandlestickStyleOptions customization (upColor, downColor, borderUpColor, borderDownColor, wickUpColor, wickDownColor) and an East Asian convention toggle (where red marks rising prices)1. ## **Local-First to Supabase Persistence, Offline Sync, and Data Integrity**

### **The iOS Safari 7-Day Script-Writable Storage Eviction Hazard**

Any local-first web application targeting iOS traders operates under a severe platform constraint: under Apple's WebKit Intelligent Tracking Prevention (ITP 2.1+), Safari purges **all script-writable storage**—IndexedDB, localStorage, sessionStorage, Service Worker registrations, and Cache Storage—after seven days of *Safari browser usage* without user interaction on that origin15. Crucially, the eviction timer counts days on which Safari is used rather than calendar days: a swing trader who browses other sites in Safari daily without opening MogMine for a week can lose every unsynced local trade and the offline Service Worker silently, with no runtime error thrown15.  
Three architectural defenses are essential. First, web applications added to the iOS Home Screen run outside Safari with their own independent usage counter, and WebKit's persistence heuristics specifically favor installed Home Screen Web Apps15. On startup, MogMine must call navigator.storage.persisted() and navigator.storage.persist(), explicitly handling false results (best-effort mode) by prompting local-only iOS users to enable cloud sync or install to Home Screen15. Second, although Safari 17+ expands origin quotas up to 60% of disk space in browser apps (80% overall; 15% origin / 20% overall in embedded webviews), storing raw base64 PNG screenshots inside IndexedDB or localStorage invites quota pressure and sluggish serialization; MogMine should serialize compact OHLC and primitive JSON state locally and store raster media in Supabase Storage112. Recording a lightweight server-side flag for returning authenticated users also exposes silent ITP wipes whenever a returning account boots into an unexpectedly empty local store16. Third, because the official Supabase JavaScript client communicates directly with the server and provides no built-in offline queue, MogMine should unify local and cloud workflows behind a single offline-first **outbox** architecture: every mutation writes optimistically to IndexedDB and enqueues a deterministic outbox record, while cloud sync acts strictly as an asynchronous replication layer draining the outbox and pulling server deltas via a lastPulledAt cursor120.

### **Conflict Resolution: Eliminating Clock Skew and Zombie Deletes**

Multi-device traders routinely log fills on iPhone during RTH and annotate the same trade on desktop after close, exposing two classic failure modes in naive Last-Write-Wins (LWW) sync implementations. Hard-deleting rows locally or remotely causes "zombie resurrection" when an offline device reconnects and re-uploads its stale copy123. Every synchronized table (accounts, trades, executions, playbooks, drills, tags) must therefore implement soft-delete **tombstones** (deleted\_at timestamptz NULL) retained beyond the maximum offline window before scheduled server compaction123. Similarly, relying on client-generated updated\_at timestamps for LWW loses data under mobile clock skew; server upserts should enforce **Optimistic Concurrency Control (OCC)** via a monotonic integer version column or server-managed updated\_at check (WHERE id \= \$1 AND version \= \$expected), surfacing true concurrent edits on prose fields (thesis or lesson notes) as non-destructive conflict copies rather than silently overwriting the trader's reasoning123.

### **Supabase PostgreSQL Security and RLS Performance Engineering**

Supabase secures PostgREST Data API access through two distinct PostgreSQL layers: **Grants** control whether a role (anon, authenticated, service\_role) can reach an object at all, while **Row Level Security (RLS)** policies filter which rows that role can read or mutate17. Auditing every migration in HEAD 37479280 requires verifying four invariants. First, every table in an exposed schema must enable RLS, explicitly revoke default privileges from anon and authenticated (as existing Supabase projects may grant wildcard privileges by default), grant back only required operations to authenticated, and define separate policies per operation (SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE) scoped with TO authenticated—keeping SELECT strictly on USING and write policies validated with WITH CHECK17. Second, writing USING (auth.uid() \= user\_id) directly evaluates auth.uid() once per scanned row, creating severe query bottlenecks as trade histories grow; wrapping it in a scalar subselect—USING ((select auth.uid()) \= user\_id)—triggers a Postgres initPlan that caches the UID once per statement, backed by composite B-tree indexes on (user\_id, trade\_date) and (user\_id, updated\_at)17. Third, PostgreSQL views default to SECURITY DEFINER execution (which can hand out rows underlying table RLS was meant to withhold), and RLS does not apply to SQL functions—requiring explicit hardening of every exposed view and strict EXECUTE privilege review on every SECURITY DEFINER RPC17. The service\_role key bypasses RLS completely and must remain isolated strictly inside trusted server-side jobs (audited with pgaudit), never exposed to browsers or untrusted agent routes17. ### **IEEE 754 Floating-Point Drift and Integer-Tick Arithmetic**

Because IEEE 754 binary floating-point numbers represent quarter-point ticks (0.25 \= 2^-2) exactly, early prototypes tested solely on MES, ES, MNQ, and NQ mask catastrophic arithmetic drift. As soon as a trader logs M2K/RTY (0.10 tick), CME FX futures such as 6E/6A (0.00005 tick), or per-contract exchange and broker fees (such as CME's \$0.37 MES exchange fee or an illustrative all-in micro commission of \$0.62 per side / \$1.24 round-trip), binary floating-point inaccuracy breaks tick validation and cumulative net P\&L1. Computational verification demonstrates both failure modes directly: on M2K, 2150.40 \- 2150.10 evaluates to 0.3000000000001819 (3.000000000001819 ticks, where symmetric negative rounding errors such as 2.9999999999998 truncate to 2 ticks under Math.floor), while summing an illustrative \$0.62 per-side fee across 3 round-trip micros yields 3.7199999999999998, and summing 100 trades at \$1.24 drifts to 123.999999999999791. MogMine must store prices as **integer ticks** (price\_ticks \= Math.round(price / tick\_size)) and monetary amounts as **integer cents** (or sub-cent mills where broker fee schedules require tenths of a cent) across TypeScript domain logic and PostgreSQL columns, deferring decimal formatting strictly to the UI layer.

## **Strategy Lab: Statistical Attribution, Multiple-Testing Hygiene, and Lookahead Prevention**

### **Lookahead Bias and Temporal Isolation Audit**

Strategy Lab's statistical attribution across playbooks, sessions, and technical regimes loses validity immediately if lookahead bias leaks future bar data into entry-time classifications25. Silent implementation bugs are remarkably pervasive even in systematic research: a ResearchGate-indexed October 2026 strategy-generation study reported that across models that read strategy specifications correctly, 79.2% of resulting implementations diverged on more than 10% of active bars—failures an automated LLM judge accepted verbatim136. Auditing Strategy Lab in HEAD 37479280 therefore requires deterministic temporal-isolation checks across four dimensions.  
First, every intraday market-context feature—anchored VWAP, session High/Low of Day (HOD/LOD), opening-range boundaries, ATR, and moving-average slopes—must be computed strictly over bars satisfying bar\_close\_time \<= entry\_timestamp; tagging a 10:15 a.m. CT entry as "Trend Day" or "Above VWAP" using 4:00 p.m. CT session-close figures injects fatal lookahead leakage25. Second, any rule-discovery or filter-tuning workflow should enforce strict chronological isolation—In-Sample (IS) for exploration, Out-of-Sample (OOS) for validation, and a frozen chronological Holdout slice evaluated once prior to live promotion26. Third, traders frequently hold simultaneous MES and MNQ positions or scale multiple tickets on the same macro impulse; counting overlapping positions as independent trades artificially inflates sample size n and narrows error bars, so Strategy Lab should measure trade-time overlap (e.g., Jaccard interval similarity) and offer a session-clustered or independent-event aggregation mode26. Fourth, attribution views must default to net-of-commission expectancy and provide a friction stress toggle (+1 tick / \+2 ticks round-trip slippage) so tight-stop scalps are tested against live CME execution drag26.

### **Small-Sample Uncertainty and Multiple-Testing False Discovery Inflation**

Retail traders routinely filter 40 trades across combinations of setup, session, day of week, and direction, uncover an 8-trade slice with a 75% win rate, and restructure their trading plan around pure noise. Exact Wilson 95% confidence-interval calculations and Family-Wise Error Rate (FWER) math illustrate why Strategy Lab must foreground statistical uncertainty rather than raw point estimates1.  
![][image4]  
To prevent Strategy Lab from encouraging backtest overfitting, MogMine should embed three statistical guardrails directly in its attribution engine. First, any filtered slice below n \= 20 should display muted typography and a "Low-N Exploratory" badge, while every win-rate readout pairs its point estimate with a 95% Wilson CI and every R-expectancy card reports a block-permutation bootstrap interval respecting serial trade dependence1. Second, multi-row attribution tables testing 10–50 playbook, time-of-day, or regime slices simultaneously must adjust raw p-values via **Benjamini-Hochberg False Discovery Rate (BH-FDR)** control before highlighting any slice as a reliable edge25. Third, for users iterating through parameter combinations, Strategy Lab should track session trial counts and compute Bailey and López de Prado's (2014) **Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR)**—which corrects both for multiple-testing selection bias and for skewed, fat-tailed futures return distributions—complemented by their non-parametric **Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO)** framework (Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu, 2017\) whenever multi-split OOS testing is enabled23.

## **Playbook Lab and Drills: Deliberate Practice, Perceptual Learning, and FSRS Scheduling**

### **Grounding Chart Drills in Deliberate Practice and Perceptual Learning**

Passively re-reading written playbooks or scrolling through completed trade screenshots creates an illusion of mastery without changing live tape execution138. Psychologist K. Anders Ericsson's research on expert performance (originating in his 1993 Berlin violinist study with Krampe and Tesch-Römer) showed that raw hours spent on comfortable repetition yield stagnation; rapid skill improvement requires **deliberate practice**: isolating a single micro-skill just beyond current ability, setting a short-term **process-oriented goal**, and receiving immediate, objective feedback27. In high-stakes visual classification fields analogous to intraday price-action recognition—including radiology, histopathology, and ECG interpretation—Philip Kellman's **Perceptual Learning Modules (PLMs)** likewise show that rapid, high-repetition visual classification trials accelerate pattern extraction and structural fluency29.  
Translating Ericsson's deliberate practice and Kellman's PLMs into MogMine's Playbook Lab and Drills dictates four interaction mechanics. Every chart drill generated from logged trades or curated playbooks must enforce **right-edge candle masking** so all bars after the decision point remain hidden until the trader commits to an active retrieval response (Setup Classification, Direction, Trigger, and Stop Level)3. Beyond textbook winners, Playbook Lab should inject **contrastive decoy cards** drawn from logged losing or skipped near-miss trades that resembled the setup superficially—training discrimination against traps rather than passive pattern recognition29. Aligning with Ericsson's finding that 20-minute high-intensity sessions outperform hours of autopilot practice, Drills should default to focused 15–20-card blocks targeting one explicit weakness (such as "Failed Breakout Traps") while tracking both classification accuracy and response latency over weeks27. Finally, post-reveal grading should use a clean 4-point rating (Again, Hard, Good, Easy) paired with clear microcopy instructing traders to tap Again whenever they misread the setup or hesitated into an invalid stop, because grading misses as Hard degrades scheduler accuracy27.

### **Upgrading Drill Scheduling from Legacy SM-2 to FSRS**

If MogMine's Drills module relies on static intervals or Piotr Woźniak's 1987 **SM-2** algorithm, it inherits SM-2's well-known **"ease hell"** flaw: repeated lapses drive a card's single ease factor down to its 1.30 floor, locking difficult chart patterns into punishingly frequent reviews even after the trader masters them, while never estimating actual recall probability30. The modern open-source alternative is the **Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler (FSRS)**, built on a three-variable **DSR** memory model tracking **Difficulty** (1–10), **Stability** (days until recall probability drops to 90%), and **Retrievability** (current recall probability on a power-law forgetting curve)30. As of late 2026, production implementations span FSRS-4.5 (17 parameters, which package maintainers report captures \~98% of FSRS-6's accuracy with lightweight Edge-runtime execution), FSRS-6 (21 parameters), and FSRS-7 (34 parameters separating short-term and long-term stability, achieving 0.3401 log loss and 0.0634 RMSE(bins) on filtered benchmark data)30.  
Evaluating FSRS objectively requires stating two caveats present in the literature: the primary open benchmark (\~350 million filtered reviews from 9,999 Anki users, drawn from \~727 million raw reviews) is maintained by the FSRS authors themselves, and the commonly cited "20–30% fewer reviews" claim comes from simulations rather than randomized controlled learning trials31. Even accounting for those limitations, empirical recall-prediction error strongly favors FSRS over SM-2—while independent workload modeling by SayMei highlights that **desired retention** and **daily new-card creation rate** influence long-term review fatigue far more than scheduler choice alone31.  
![][image5]  
MogMine should therefore adopt FSRS-4.5 or FSRS-6 with a default desired retention of **85%–90%**, cap user-selectable retention at **≤95%** with an explicit daily-workload preview above 92%, keep intraday relearning steps short (10–30 minutes), and offer an optional monthly per-user optimizer rather than manual parameter editing31.

## **Agent API v1: Security Architecture, Guardrails, and Human-AI Trust Calibration**

### **API Security Controls (OWASP API Top 10:2023 and Web Top 10:2025)**

Exposing an Agent API v1 allowing external LLMs, MCP servers, or automated scripts to query trades, read Strategy Lab outputs, and append journal reviews creates a high-stakes financial security perimeter. Auditing HEAD 37479280 against the OWASP API Security Top 10 (2023) and OWASP Top 10 (2025) requires enforcing five deterministic server-side controls32.  
First, **cryptographic API key lifecycle (API2:2023)** must prohibit plaintext database storage and reject keys passed in URL query strings (which leak into server logs and referers)150. Keys should carry at least 256 bits of random entropy behind an identifiable prefix (e.g., mog\_live\_\<43 base64url chars\>), display once at creation, store only a short unsecret lookup prefix plus a **SHA-256** digest (avoiding slow password hashes like bcrypt/Argon2, which are unnecessary for high-entropy random secrets and invite CPU exhaustion), and verify incoming digests via crypto.timingSafeEqual to prevent timing attacks151. Supporting two concurrent active keys per user enables zero-downtime rotation alongside revocation timestamps and last\_used\_at auditing152.  
Second, **object-level, property-level, and function-level authorization (API1, API3, API5:2023)** must bind every resource lookup (trade\_id, account\_id, playbook\_id, drill\_id) to the authenticated key's user\_id inside user-scoped queries to eliminate Broken Object Level Authorization (BOLA)149. All request and response schemas must enforce strict DTO allowlists to block **BOPLA**—preventing mass assignment of privileged fields (user\_id, initial\_r\_cents, billing flags) on write and excessive internal data exposure on read—while enforcing granular per-key scopes (trades:read, trades:write, analytics:read, drills:write, omitting destructive delete scopes by default)149.  
Third, **rate limiting and cost circuit breakers (API4:2023)** must throttle per API key and per IP while returning standard headers (X-RateLimit-Limit, X-RateLimit-Remaining, X-RateLimit-Window, X-RateLimit-Reset), paired with compute and token caps for heavy Strategy Lab aggregations or LLM-backed routes33. Fourth, **fail-closed exception handling (A10:2025)** requires that any failure in auth middleware, scope checks, or rate-limit lookups rejects requests cleanly (401/403/503) without leaking stack traces, SQL errors, or submitted keys148. Fifth, **mutation safety via IETF Idempotency-Key** protects non-idempotent POST and PATCH routes against automated agent retry storms35. Keys (client-generated UUIDv4) must be scoped to (user\_id, endpoint, key) and claimed *before* business logic via an atomic PostgreSQL unique insert (INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING)35. Concurrent in-flight retries return 409 Conflict, completed retries matching the request payload SHA-256 fingerprint replay the stored 2xx status and body, mismatched payloads reject with 422 Unprocessable Content, and transient 503/504 errors are never cached as final outcomes35.
